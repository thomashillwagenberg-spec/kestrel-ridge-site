## What we build

Kestrel Ridge Health builds staffing software for multi-site ophthalmology practices. The product forecasts technician workload by session and assigns staff under eight hard rules that encode how experienced administrators already think about coverage. The output is a staffing plan recommendation, not a dashboard to interpret.

The forecast uses historical session data to predict the technician minutes each session will need, from its appointment mix and complexity. Eight hard rules sit on top of the forecast: required skills, availability, approved sites, no double booking, travel time between sites, weekly hours, minimum staffing and trainee supervision. Procedure mix shapes the forecast; provider preferences shape the plan where the rules allow. A human approves every recommendation before it affects any schedule.

## Why we started

Ophthalmic technicians are credentialed clinical staff. They are not interchangeable with medical assistants or general clinical support. Training takes months. The scope of work spans pre-testing, patient education, surgical prep, and specialty imaging. Multi-site groups face compounding complexity: sessions vary by subspecialty, provider volume, and site capability. A group running four locations with a mix of general ophthalmology, retina, and cataract surgery may be managing 30 or more distinct session types across the week.

The staffing problem at that scale is not solved by hiring more. It is solved by knowing, in advance, which sessions are likely to run short and why. That is the gap Kestrel Ridge Health is built to close.

## Current status

Phase Zero is available now. It is a diagnostic engagement that runs on a clinic's existing schedule data. It produces a workload forecast by session type, identifies the sessions with the highest variance, and scores the forecast against both your current method and a historical average. Phase Zero does not require new software, integration work, or staff training to run.

Groups that complete Phase Zero receive a written report and a structured recommendation for whether and how to proceed to a staffed schedule product. There is no automatic progression to a further engagement. The diagnostic stands on its own.
