sm:pt-24" data-reveal style="opacity:1;transform:none"><h2 class="max-w-3xl font-serif text-2xl leading-[1.14] sm:text-[2rem]">The three numbers everyone quotes, and where they came from</h2><div class="mt-6"><ol class="surface-lead mt-6 px-5 sm:px-7 md:px-8"><li class="py-8 sm:py-10 "><h3 class="max-w-[20ch] font-mono text-[1.4375rem] font-medium leading-[1.18] tracking-[-0.025em] tabular-nums text-ink sm:text-[1.75rem]">4 to 8 FTE staff per FTE provider</h3><div class="mt-1"><p class="prose-measure mt-4 text-base text-ink-muted">This range appears in<!-- --> <a href="https://crstoday.com/articles/2016-oct/what-is-the-right-ratio" class="text-primary underline underline-offset-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">CRSToday’s “What Is the Right Ratio?”</a> <!-- -->(October 2016), attributed to American Academy of Ophthalmology benchmarking data and reported as the 25th to 75th percentile band.</p><p class="prose-measure mt-4 text-base text-ink-muted">Two things about this number are routinely lost in retelling. First, it counts all staff, clinical and administrative, not technicians alone. If you are trying to size a technician team specifically, this range does not answer your question. Second, a 25th-to-75th-percentile band is a description of what practices do, not a statement of what works. Half of all practices sit outside it, and the article gives no evidence that the ones inside it perform better.</p></div></li><li class="py-8 sm:py-10 border-t border-rule"><h3 class="max-w-[20ch] font-mono text-[1.4375rem] font-medium leading-[1.18] tracking-[-0.025em] tabular-nums text-ink sm:text-[1.75rem]">3 to 3.5 patients per technician per hour</h3><div class="mt-1"><p class="prose-measure mt-4 text-base text-ink-muted">Also from the CRSToday piece, attributed to American Society of Ophthalmic Administrators technician benchmarking studies conducted in 2009 with a follow-up in 2015. The article is explicit that this assumes “a balanced schedule combination of long and short exams,” and notes that technicians in retina and oculoplastics may work up more per hour.</p><p class="prose-measure mt-4 text-base text-ink-muted">The conditional clause is the whole point and it is almost always dropped. The number is an average across a mix. It tells you nothing about a session that is not that mix, which is most sessions.</p></div></li><li class="py-8 sm:py-10 border-t border-rule"><h3 class="max-w-[20ch] font-mono text-[1.4375rem] font-medium leading-[1.18] tracking-[-0.025em] tabular-nums text-ink sm:text-[1.75rem]">1 physician to 1.26 technical staff</h3><div class="mt-1"><p class="prose-measure mt-4 text-base text-ink-muted">This is the most-repeated ratio in ophthalmology practice management, and it is the oldest. It originates in<!-- --> <a href="https://www.reviewofophthalmology.com/article/benchmarking-a-better-payroll" class="text-primary underline underline-offset-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Review of Ophthalmology’s “Benchmarking A Better Payroll”</a> <!-- -->by Sandra E. D. McGraw and Kathryn E. Kocevar, published 15 January 2003, drawing on 2002 ASOA benchmarking survey data.</p><p class="prose-measure mt-4 text-base text-ink-muted">That data is from 2002. Since then ophthalmology practices have adopted electronic health records, added substantial diagnostic imaging volume, and changed their scribing arrangements. The 2002 ratio predates all of it. It is still quoted as though it were current.</p></div></li></ol></div></section><section id="good-for" class="section-edge rule-top pb-10 pt-16 sm:pb-14 sm:pt-24" data-reveal style="opacity:1;transform:none"><h2 class="max-w-3xl font-serif text-2xl leading-[1.14] sm:text-[2rem]">What the benchmarks are genuinely good for</h2><div class="prose-measure mt-2.5 text-base text-ink-muted"><p>Ratios are the right tool for a small, specific set of decisions.</p></div><div class="mt-10 sm:mt-12"><ul class="surface mt-6 space-y-3 py-6 pl-10 pr-6 text-base leading-relaxed text-ink-muted [&amp;&gt;li]:max-w-[68ch] list-disc"><li><strong class="font-semibold text-ink">Annual headcount budgeting.</strong> <!-- -->If you need a defensible starting number for next year’s technician FTE line, a ratio applied to projected provider FTE is a reasonable first pass.</li><li><strong class="font-semibold text-ink">Detecting a gross outlier.</strong> <!-- -->If you are running 12 staff per provider or 2, the benchmark is telling you something real and you should go find out what.</li><li><strong class="font-semibold text-ink">Payroll as a share of revenue.</strong> <a href="https://ophthalmologymanagement.com/issues/2011/april/do-i-have-the-right-number-of-staff/" class="text-primary underline underline-offset-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Ophthalmology Management’s “Do I Have the Right Number of Staff?”</a> <!-- -->uses gross wages at roughly 25 percent of practice revenues as a check. That is a financial control, and it works as one.</li><li><strong class="font-semibold text-ink">Arguing with a physician about a hiring request.</strong> <!-- -->An external benchmark is a neutral third party in a conversation that otherwise runs on anecdote.</li></ul></div></section><section id="cannot-decide" class="section-edge rule-top pb-10 pt-16 sm:pb-14 sm:pt-24" data-reveal style="opacity:1;transform:none"><h2 class="max-w-3xl font-serif text-2xl leading-[1.14] sm:text-[2rem]">What ratios structurally cannot decide</h2><div class="prose-measure mt-2.5 text-base text-ink-muted"><p>A ratio has one input, provider count, and one output, staff count. Anything that varies while provider count stays constant is invisible to it. In an ophthalmology clinic, almost everything varies while provider count stays constant.</p></div><div class="mt-10 sm:mt-12"><p class="mt-9 max-w-[24ch] border-t-2 border-rule-strong pt-5 font-serif text-[1.625rem] leading-[1.16] text-ink sm:text-[2rem]">A ratio sees ten patients.</p><div class="panel-grid surface mt-6 grid gap-px sm:grid-cols-2"><div class="bg-card px-5 py-6 sm:px-6 sm:py-7 "><h3 class="font-serif text-base leading-snug text-ink sm:text-lg">It cannot see appointment mix</h3><div class="prose-measure mt-2.5 text-[0.9375rem] leading-relaxed text-ink-muted"><p>Ten patients is not ten patients. A ten-patient session of postoperative cataract checks and a ten-patient session of new glaucoma workups with visual fields, OCT, and pachymetry consume very different amounts of technician time. Both are ten patients.</p></div></div><div class="bg-card px-5 py-6 sm:px-6 sm:py-7 "><h3 class="font-serif text-base leading-snug text-ink sm:text-lg">It cannot see diagnostic testing load</h3><div class="prose-measure mt-2.5 text-[0.9375rem] leading-relaxed text-ink-muted"><p>Diagnostic testing is where technician minutes actually go, and testing load varies by subspecialty, by provider habit, and by individual patient. The CRSToday piece quotes Maureen Waddle of BSM Consulting directly on this: she states that despite the existence of employees-per-physician benchmarks, she does not believe it is the best way to plan a staffing model, citing specialty type, diagnostic test volume, and provider type as material factors.</p><p>Waddle made the same argument at length in the 2011 Ophthalmology Management article, proposing that staffing be modelled from three work units, number of office visits, number of diagnostic tests, and number of surgeries, rather than from provider count. That article is now fifteen years old and remains the strongest published statement of the case.</p></div></div><div class="bg-card px-5 py-6 sm:px-6 sm:py-7 "><h3 class="font-serif text-base leading-snug text-ink sm:text-lg">It cannot see credentialing constraints</h3><div class="prose-measure mt-2.5 text-[0.9375rem] leading-relaxed text-ink-muted"><p>Technician minutes are not fungible. A COA, a COT, and a COMT are different credentials with different scopes, documented by <a href="https://www.jcahpo.org/certification/" class="text-primary underline underline-offset-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">IJCAHPO</a>, which also certifies ophthalmic surgical assisting, ophthalmic ultrasound biometry, diagnostic ophthalmic sonography, and ophthalmic scribes. Site privileges, device competencies, and hour restrictions layer on top. A ratio that says you need 6.4 technicians has said nothing about whether the six you have can legally and competently do the specific work on Tuesday’s schedule.</p></div></div><div class="bg-card px-5 py-6 sm:px-6 sm:py-7 "><h3 class="font-serif text-base leading-snug text-ink sm:text-lg">It cannot see the variance it is averaging over</h3><div class="prose-measure mt-2.5 text-[0.9375rem] leading-relaxed text-ink-muted"><p>This is the deepest limitation. A ratio is a mean. Staffing to the mean means being right on average and wrong most days, over on the light ones and under on the heavy ones. The cost of being wrong is asymmetric: an overstaffed session costs you a technician’s hourly wage, and an understaffed session costs you patient wait time, provider idle time, technician burnout, and sometimes a rescheduled patient.</p><p>For context on that hourly wage,<!-- --> <a href="https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-2057.00" class="text-primary underline underline-offset-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">O*NET reports</a> a 2025 median of $21.91 hourly for ophthalmic medical technicians, with 78,800 employed as of 2024. The<!-- --> <a href="https://www.bls.gov/oes/2022/may/oes292057.htm" class="text-primary underline underline-offset-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics for May 2022</a> <!-- -->put employment at 66,060 with a mean annual wage of $42,480 and a 10th-to-90th-percentile band of $29,830 to $58,970. The point of the wage figure is not that overstaffing is cheap. It is that the two failure modes have different units, and a single ratio cannot balance them.</p></div></div></div></div></section><section id="division-of-labour" class="section-edge rule-top pb-10 class="prose-measure mt-2.5 text-base text-ink-muted"><p>If you are using ratios today, three changes make them more useful without adopting anything new.</p></div><div class="mt-10 sm:mt-12"><ol
