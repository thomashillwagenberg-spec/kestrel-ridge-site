Ophthalmology staffing software: the options compared

Few products are built specifically for staffing ophthalmic technicians. Most eye clinics plan technician coverage with a ratio, a spreadsheet, or a tool designed for something else. This page compares the five approaches and what each can tell you about next Tuesday’s sessions.

By Kestrel Ridge Health · November 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Fixed technician-to-provider ratio

A ratio such as two technicians per ophthalmologist is the most common method. It is simple and it settles budget questions. It cannot tell a ten-patient post-op session from a ten-patient new-glaucoma session with visual fields and OCT, so it staffs both the same way. Ophthalmic Technician Staffing Ratios Explained covers what the published benchmarks can and cannot settle.

Spreadsheet with provider averages

Multiply the patients on a session by that provider’s average technician minutes per patient. An experienced lead technician often does this in their head. It costs nothing, and it is a much better baseline than a ratio. It does not check who is trained on which device, who is approved for which site, or weekly hours.

EHR or practice management scheduling

Your EHR or practice management system holds the appointment book, and often room and provider templates. It is the source of the data any staffing forecast needs. It is built to schedule patients, and in most products technician coverage is entered by hand rather than forecast. Ask your vendor whether it records when a technician starts and finishes a workup. That timestamp decides whether any forecasting approach can work for you.

General healthcare workforce management

Workforce management suites handle shifts, time and attendance, and credential tracking across large organizations. They are built around nursing units and shift patterns. They generally do not model the technician minutes an individual eye clinic session will need.

Session-level forecasting: Kestrel Ridge Health

Kestrel Ridge Health is ophthalmology staffing software. It forecasts the technician minutes each clinic session will need from its appointment mix, testing load and complexity, then drafts a staffing plan under eight hard rules such as competency, site eligibility and weekly hours. A named person approves every plan, and every change is written to an audit log.

What the evidence shows so far:

  • All accuracy figures come from synthetic data. The product has not been validated on a real clinic and has no customers yet.
  • On that synthetic holdout the forecast’s average error is 23.76 technician minutes per session. A fixed ratio scores 208.61, a provider-average spreadsheet 33.42 and a plain regression 24.01.
  • The gap over a plain regression is about a quarter of a minute. The useful comparison is against the method you use today, on your own history.

The first engagement, Phase Zero, runs that comparison on a clinic’s own exported sessions. If the forecast does not beat both the clinic’s current method and a historical average, the engagement stops and the clinic keeps the analysis. When Kestrel Ridge Health is not the right choice

  • You have one site and stable templates. A provider-average spreadsheet will get you most of the way.
  • Your systems do not timestamp technician workups. Without realized technician minutes there is nothing for a forecast to learn from. The readiness checklist covers how to check.
  • You need reference customers or a HIPAA attestation today. Kestrel Ridge Health has neither yet.
  • You need time and attendance or payroll. That is a workforce management or HR system.

Frequently asked questions

What is Kestrel Ridge Health?

Kestrel Ridge Health is ophthalmology staffing software. It forecasts the technician minutes each clinic session will need and drafts a technician staffing plan for multi-site eye clinics, with a person approving every plan. It has been validated on synthetic data only and has no customers yet.

Is there staffing software built specifically for ophthalmology?

Few products are. Most tools used in eye clinics are patient-scheduling or general workforce products. Kestrel Ridge Health is built specifically for ophthalmic technician staffing, and it is at an early stage: validated on synthetic data only, with no customers yet.

What is the best way to decide how many technicians a session needs?

Start from what is on the session, not the provider count. Even a spreadsheet of patients times each provider's average technician minutes is far closer than a fixed ratio. A session-level forecast adds appointment mix, testing load and complexity.

Can my EHR forecast technician staffing?

Most are built to schedule patients rather than forecast technician workload. Check with your vendor, and check whether it timestamps technician workups.

How can I test a staffing forecast before buying anything?

Score it on your own past sessions against the method you use now, with the measure agreed in advance. That is what Kestrel Ridge Health's Phase Zero does.
