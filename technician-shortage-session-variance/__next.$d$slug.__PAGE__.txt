class="min-w-0 lg:col-start-1 lg:row-start-1"><section id="supply-side" class="section-edge rule-top pb-10 pt-16 sm:pb-14 sm:pt-24" data-reveal style="opacity:1;transform:none"><h2 class="max-w-3xl font-serif text-2xl leading-[1.14] sm:text-[2rem]">The supply side is real, and it is not going to move quickly</h2><div class="prose-measure mt-2.5 text-base text-ink-muted"><p>The scale of the constraint is well documented and worth stating precisely, because the numbers get inflated in retelling.</p></div><div class="mt-10 sm:mt-12"><p class="prose-measure mt-4 text-base text-ink-muted"><a href="https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-2057.00" class="text-primary underline underline-offset-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">O*NET reports</a> 78,800 ophthalmic medical technicians employed in the United States as of 2024, with employment projected to grow much faster than average, 7 percent or higher, over 2024 to 2034, and approximately 12,500 openings projected across that decade. The<!-- --> <a href="https://www.bls.gov/oes/2022/may/oes292057.htm" class="text-primary underline underline-offset-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics for May 2022</a> <!-- -->recorded 66,060 employed at a mean annual wage of $42,480, with 39,880 of those in offices of physicians.</p><p class="prose-measure mt-4 text-base text-ink-muted">On the ophthalmology side,<!-- --> <a href="https://www.aao.org/eyenet/article/ophthalmic-technicians-tackling-the-tech-shortage" class="text-primary underline underline-offset-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">AAO EyeNet reported in May 2021</a> fewer than 60,000 ophthalmic technicians supporting more than 19,000 practising ophthalmologists. Ruth D. Williams, MD, writing in<!-- --> <a href="https://www.aao.org/eyenet/article/coping-with-staffing-shortages" class="text-primary underline underline-offset-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">EyeNet in December 2021</a>, put it at 59,960 technicians against roughly 18,000 ophthalmologists, about 3.3 per physician, and noted the shortage predates the pandemic.</p><p class="prose-measure mt-4 text-base text-ink-muted">The training pipeline is the binding constraint and it is not expanding.<!-- --> <a href="https://www.reviewofophthalmology.com/article/the-ophthalmic-staffing-shortage" class="text-primary underline underline-offset-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Review of Ophthalmology reported in October 2019</a> <!-- -->about 30 accredited programs across 19 states plus the District of Columbia. Four years later,<!-- --> <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10789446/" class="text-primary underline underline-offset-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Mai et al. in Ophthalmology Glaucoma</a> reported approximately 30 certified training programs and noted the count had not changed despite rising demand. New efforts exist, including a short-course program described in<!-- --> <a href="https://crstoday.com/crst-issues/june-2025/a-workforce-solution-to-expand-access-to-eye-care/45509/" class="text-primary underline underline-offset-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">CRSToday in June 2025</a>, but no published outcome data on placement or retention was available at the time of writing.</p><p class="prose-measure mt-4 text-base text-ink-muted">The practical conclusion for an administrator is not despair. It is that supply-side effort should continue and should not be the only thing you do, because none of it changes next month.</p></div></section><section id="demand-side" class="section-edge rule-top pb-10 pt-16 sm:pb-14 sm:pt-24" data-reveal style="opacity:1;transform:none"><h2 class="max-w-3xl font-serif text-2xl leading-[1.14] sm:text-[2rem]">The demand side nobody writes about</h2><div class="prose-measure mt-2.5 text-base text-ink-muted"><p>Every session in your clinic consumes a different quantity of technician minutes, and most practices staff them as though they did not.</p></div><div class="mt-10 sm:mt-12"><p class="mt-9 max-w-[24ch] border-t-2 border-rule-strong pt-5 font-serif text-[1.625rem] leading-[1.16] text-ink sm:text-[2rem]">Ten patients is not ten patients.</p><p class="prose-measure mt-4 text-base text-ink-muted">A postoperative cataract session and a new-glaucoma workup session with visual fields, OCT, pachymetry and extended counselling are different jobs wearing the same patient count. Staff both from a ratio and you are over on one and under on the other, systematically, every week.</p><p class="prose-measure mt-4 text-base text-ink-muted">This is arithmetically unavoidable if you staff from a mean. A mean is right on average and wrong on most individual days. The published benchmarks are explicit about this if you read the conditions attached. The<!-- --> <a href="https://crstoday.com/articles/2016-oct/what-is-the-right-ratio" class="text-primary underline underline-offset-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ASOA throughput figure of 3 to 3.5 patients per technician per hour</a>, cited in CRSToday from studies of 2009 and 2015, is stated as assuming “a balanced schedule combination of long and short exams.” Most sessions are not a balanced combination. They are a block of one thing.</p><h3 class="mt-10 font-serif text-lg leading-snug text-ink sm:text-xl">The two failure modes cost different things</h3><div class="panel-grid surface mt-6 grid gap-px sm:grid-cols-2"><div class="bg-card px-5 py-6 sm:px-6 sm:py-7 "><p class="font-serif text-base font-semibold leading-snug text-ink sm:text-lg">Overstaffed session.</p><div class="prose-measure mt-2.5 text-[0.9375rem] leading-relaxed text-ink-muted"><p>You pay a technician’s hourly wage for capacity you did not use. O*NET puts the 2025 median at $21.91 hourly. The cost is real, bounded, and appears in payroll.</p></div></div><div class="bg-card px-5 py-6 sm:px-6 sm:py-7 "><p class="font-serif text-base font-semibold leading-snug text-ink sm:text-lg">Understaffed session.</p><div class="prose-measure mt-2.5 text-[0.9375rem] leading-relaxed text-ink-muted"><p>Patients wait. The provider waits between rooms. Technicians absorb the gap by working through breaks. Workup quality degrades under time pressure. Some of that cost lands on next month’s turnover, which returns you to the supply-side problem you were already losing.</p></div></div></div><p class="prose-measure mt-4 text-base text-ink-muted">These costs are not symmetric and they are not in the same units, which is precisely why a single ratio cannot balance them. It is also why administrators over-hedge upward when they can afford to, and why they cannot afford to in a shortage.</p><h3 class="mt-10 font-serif text-lg leading-snug text-ink sm:text-xl">The friction compounds, and it drives people out</h3><p class="prose-measure mt-4 text-base text-ink-muted">The demand-side and supply-side problems are connected, and Mai et al. make the connection explicitly. Their argument in Ophthalmology Glaucoma is that workflow inefficiency, not compensation alone, drives technician turnover: time lost transferring data among systems and moving patients between examination rooms and testing devices is time not spent on the parts of the job technicians find worthwhile. Their prescription is device integration and automated documentation.</p><p class="prose-measure mt-4 text-base text-ink-muted">Chronic understaffing on heavy sessions is the same category of friction and it is not addressed by better devices. A technician who is under-resourced on Tuesdays every week is being asked to absorb a planning failure with their own effort. That is a retention problem generated by an allocation problem.</p></div></section><section id="sizing" class="section-edge rule-top pb-10 pt-16 sm:pb-14 sm:pt-24" data-reveal style="opacity:1;transform:none"><h2 class="max-w-3xl font-serif text-2xl leading-[1.14] sm:text-[2rem]">How to size the problem on your own data</h2><div class="prose-measure mt-2.5 text-base text-ink-muted"><p>This does not require a vendor, a purchase, or a pilot. It requires an export and an afternoon.</p></div><div class="mt-10 sm:mt-12"><ol class="surface mt-6 px-5 sm:px-7"><li class="py-7 sm:py-8 "><h3 class="font-serif text-lg leading-snug text-ink">Step 1: establish whether you can measure it at all</h3><div class="prose-measure mt-2.5 text-[0.9375rem] leading-relaxed text-ink-muted"><p>Does your practice management system or EHR timestamp when a technician begins and ends a patient workup? If yes, you can do everything below. If no, that instrumentation is the actual first project, and it comes before any conversation about forecasting or software. This is the most common place practices discover they are not ready, and finding out costs nothing.</p></div></li><li class="py-7 sm:py-8 border-t border-rule"><h3 class="font-serif text-lg leading-snug text-ink">Step 2: pull twelve months of sessions</h3><div class="prose-measure mt-2.5 text-[0.9375rem] leading-relaxed text-ink-muted"><p>For each historical clinic session: date, site, provider, appointment counts by type, diagnostic tests performed, and realized technician minutes.</p></div></li><li class="py-7 sm:py-8 border-t border-rule"><h3 class="font-serif text-lg leading-snug text-ink">Step 3: plot realized technician minutes against patient count</h3><div class="prose-measure mt-2.5 text-[0.9375rem] leading-relaxed text-ink-muted"><p>If the points sit close to a line, your patient count predicts your technician need, and a ratio is serving you adequately. Stop here; you do not have this problem.</p><p class="mt-4">If the scatter is wide, the vertical spread at any given patient count is the size of your problem, measured in minutes, on your own operation. That is a number you can take to a physician partner.</p></div></li><li class="py-7 sm:py-8 border-t border-rule"><h3 class="font-serif text-lg leading-snug text-ink">Step 4: compute your current baseline error</h3><div class="prose-measure mt-2.5 text-[0.9375rem] leading-relaxed text-ink-muted"><p>For each session, calculate what your current staffing rule would have predicted and how far off it was. That single number is your baseline, and every future claim by anyone, including any software vendor, gets measured against it.</p><p class="mt-4">Doing step 4 before talking to anyone is the most valuable hour in this entire process, because it converts a vendor demo from a story into an arithmetic comparison.</p></div></li></ol></div></section><section id="multi-site" class="section-edge rule-top pb-10 pt-16 sm:pb-14 sm:pt-24" data-reveal style="opacity:1;transform:none"><h2 class="max-w-3xl font-serif text-2xl leading-[1.14] sm:text-[2rem]">Multi-site is where the variance becomes an opportunity</h2><div class="prose-measure mt-2.5 text-base text-ink-muted"><p>A single-site practice with a heavy Tuesday has a hard problem: the minutes are not there. A multi-site group with a heavy Tuesday at one location often has a light Tuesday at another, and the minutes exist somewhere in the organisation.
